# B1.1 Build It In-House or Hire It Out

> ⚖️ **Both work. They cost different things.**
>
> This page is the honest comparison, including the cases where hiring it out is plainly the right answer.

## 📊 Side by Side

|  | 🏠 In-house | 🤝 Hired out |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Time to first meeting** | 6 to 8 weeks from a standing start | Often days, if they run pre-warmed infrastructure |
| **Your time** | A working week to set up, then 15 minutes a day plus replies | An onboarding call, then the replies |
| **Monthly cost** | Tools only. Small | A fee, plus tools if they are not included |
| **Cost per meeting over time** | Falls. Assets persist and the ratio improves | Flat, unless the arrangement changes |
| **Who learns** | You. Permanently | Them, mostly |
| **Speed of change** | Immediate. Change the offer on a Tuesday | The right agency can implement changes fast. Their experience help make the right changes |
| **When it breaks** | Your problem, your domains | Their problem, their domains |

## 🏠 What Building It Actually Gives You

Three things you cannot buy.

**The data stays yours.** Your list, your suppression, your history of who was contacted when. If an arrangement ends, you keep everything.

**The learning compounds.** After six months of this you understand your market better than any supplier ever will. That shows up in your pricing, your sales conversations and your service, not just in your emails.

**The cost per meeting falls.** Month one is the most expensive month you will ever have. By month six the infrastructure is bought, the list is built, the copy is written, and the marginal cost of another meeting is very low.

## 🤝 What Hiring It Out Actually Gives You

Three things that are genuinely hard to replicate.

**Speed.** The two to six week wait for warm infrastructure disappears if somebody already has warmed inboxes running. That is the single biggest practical difference.

**Somebody else owns the failure.** When a domain burns or a provider changes a protocol, it is their weekend.

**Pattern recognition.** Somebody running many programs sees a provider having a bad week, or a warm-up protocol that stopped working, far faster than any single sender can.

## ✅ When Hiring Out Is Plainly Right

Said plainly, because a guide that never says this is a sales page.

- ⏰ **You do not have a working week to spend on setup**, and you will not find one
- 🧰 **You have no appetite for the technical layer**, and that is a preference rather than a gap
- 🏃 **You need pipeline this quarter**, not in two months
- 🔁 **You tried, and it stalled** at infrastructure or at reply handling
- 🧮 **The maths says a specialist costs less per meeting** than your own hours

## 🏠 When Building It Is Plainly Right

- 🧠 **You want to own the capability**, because it is core to how you grow
- 🕐 **You have the time and some interest** in the mechanics
- 💰 **Your deal size is small enough** that a monthly fee eats the margin
- 🔬 **Your offer changes often** and you need to move faster than a supplier can
- 🗺️ **Your market is unusual enough** that you know it better than anyone you could hire

## 🔀 The Hybrid Most People End Up At

Not binary, and this is where a lot of sensible businesses land.

- 🏗️ **Have somebody build it, then run it yourself.** Setup is the hard, technical, one-off part. Running it is fifteen minutes a day
- 📞 **Keep the campaigns, outsource the calling.** If the only broken part is the reply window, fix only that
- 🗺️ **Buy the data, build everything else.** The resolution layer is the hardest thing to replicate and the easiest thing to buy
- 🚀 **Hire out to start, bring it in later.** Get pipeline now, learn from what they built, take it over once it works

**Hand over the specific thing that is hard for you.** Not the whole function by default.

## 🧭 How to Decide in Five Minutes

Three questions, answered honestly.

1. **Do I have a working week in the next fortnight?** If no, that answers most of it
2. **Counting my time at what it is worth, which is cheaper per meeting?** Take your monthly tool cost, add your hours at a real hourly rate, and divide by meetings held. Compare that to a monthly fee
3. **Do I want to own this capability, or do I want the meetings?** Both are legitimate

If you want the meetings and not the capability, hire it out and stop feeling bad about it. If you want the capability, the rest of this guide is how.
