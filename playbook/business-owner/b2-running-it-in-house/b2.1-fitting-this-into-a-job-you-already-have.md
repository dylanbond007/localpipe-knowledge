# B2.1 Fitting This Into a Job You Already Have

> ⏰ **Fifteen minutes a day, an hour a week, half a day a month.**
>
> That is the real running cost once it is live. The setup is heavier, and the replies are the part nobody budgets for.

## 📅 The Actual Time Commitment

| When | How long | What you are doing |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Setup** | Most of a working week, spread over 15 days | Infrastructure, list, offer, copy, QA |
| **Daily** | 15 minutes | Bounce, reply rate, and answering replies |
| **Weekly** | 1 hour | Placement test, batch rotation, lagging numbers |
| **Monthly** | Half a day | Review, scale or kill decisions, next month's list |

**The replies are extra**, and they are the part that actually varies. Seventeen positive replies a month, each needing a call inside the hour, is not fifteen minutes a day. It is closer to three or four hours a month of genuinely interruptive time.

## 🎯 What the Fifteen Minutes Is For

Not admin. Two numbers and one action.

- 💥 **Bounce rate** against the 2% bar
- 📬 **Reply rate** against your own baseline
- 📞 **Working whatever came in**

That is it. Everything else waits for the weekly slot. The daily check exists to catch a problem in its first day rather than its second week, not to give you something to fiddle with.

## 🚫 What Happens When You Skip a Week

Not dramatic, which is why it happens.

- 📉 Bounce drifts up and nobody notices until it is at 4%
- ❄️ Replies sit for three days and go cold. Section 11.1
- 🔥 A domain starts slipping and keeps sending while it does
- 🗑️ The list ages, and you send to data that was fresh a month ago

**Recovery costs more than maintenance would have.** A fortnight of neglect is usually a month of rebuilding, and on a finite market some of what you burned is not coming back for six months.

## 📦 Batch It

The single best adaptation for somebody with a day job.

Rather than running a continuous drip that demands attention every day, launch in **weekly batches**. Load Monday, run through the week, review Friday.

- 🗓️ You know when the replies will arrive, so you can plan around them
- 📊 A bad week is visible instead of averaged away
- 🧠 It matches how a busy person actually works, in blocks rather than continuously

## 🤖 What Automates and What Never Will

> ✅ **Automates cleanly**
>
> Sourcing and enrichment.
>
> Sending and sequencing.
>
> Warm-up and rotation.
>
> Reminders and confirmations.
>
> Reporting.

> ❌ **Never will**
>
> The call after a reply.
>
> Deciding what the numbers mean.
>
> The offer.
>
> Judgment about which prospects are worth more effort.

The thing that cannot be automated is also the thing that decides whether any of it produces revenue. Plan your time around the reply, not around the build.

## ⚠️ The Failure Mode

Setting it up brilliantly, then not looking at it for a month.

It is extremely common, and it is not laziness. The setup is interesting and the maintenance is boring, so attention drifts the moment the novelty goes.

Two defences:

- 📌 **Put the fifteen minutes in the calendar at a fixed time**, attached to something you already do
- 🔔 **Make replies interrupt you.** Push notification to your phone, not an email you check later

If the daily check is not in a calendar, it will not happen in week six.

## ✅ Before You Move On

- Fifteen minutes booked at a fixed time each day
- Replies arriving as a phone notification
- Weekly batches rather than a continuous drip
- An honest estimate of reply-handling time on top of the maintenance
