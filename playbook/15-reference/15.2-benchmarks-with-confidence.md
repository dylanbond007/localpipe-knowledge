## 🏷️ The Three Kinds of Number

| Type | What it means | How much to trust it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 📊 **Measured at scale** | From LocalPipe's analysis of roughly 7.2 million emails sent to local businesses | High on direction. One company's dataset, not an industry survey |
| 🧪 **Controlled comparison** | Four data providers tested against the same lists, with offer and infrastructure held steady | The cleanest evidence here, and the basis of the find rates |
| 🛠️ **Practitioner figures** | From Legacy GTM running these programs, across 20 million or more cold emails sent | Real, but from our programs. Informed expectations rather than proven averages |

## ⚠️ Known Conflicts, and How This Guide Resolves Them

Three places where you will find different advice elsewhere.

**Bounce threshold.** General cold email advice often treats 3% as acceptable. This guide uses **2%** throughout, because local data is built rather than bought, so a high bounce rate is almost always something you did and can fix.

**Contacts per business.** Standard practice caps you at around five contacts per company. This guide works two to three contacts per business, because those are multiple routes to the same person rather than multiple people at one account. Section 6.4. **This is the most contested position in the guide and there is no controlled study behind it.**

**Free-provider addresses.** Standard practice deletes them. This guide isolates them, because on a local record they usually reach the owner personally. Section 4.6.

## 🧭 How to Use All of This

- 📐 **Plan against the controlled figures.** The find rates are the most reliable thing here
- 🤔 **Treat practitioner figures as starting assumptions** and replace them with your own by day ninety
- 🚫 **Do not average across verticals.** Plumbers and dental practices behave differently, and a blended number hides a working campaign inside a failing portfolio
- 🔍 **The spread between approaches is wider than the spread between verticals.** A bad process in a good vertical loses to a good process in a hard one
