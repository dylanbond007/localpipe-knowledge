# 15.4 Compliance

> ⚠️ **This is operational guidance, not legal advice.**
>
> Rules differ by country, by state and by the kind of address you are writing to, and they change. Nothing on this page is a substitute for somebody qualified in your jurisdiction. What it does is tell you which questions to ask and which habits keep you out of trouble.

## 🌍 The Shape of It

Most places that regulate commercial email land on a similar set of expectations for business-to-business contact.

- 🪪 **Be identifiable.** A real sender, a real business, a real address somewhere in the message
- 🎯 **Be relevant.** Contacting somebody about something plausibly connected to their business is treated very differently from bulk consumer mail
- 🚪 **Offer a way out**, and honour it immediately
- 🚫 **Do not disguise what it is.** No misleading subject lines, no fake reply chains, no pretending you have met

Jurisdictions vary considerably in how strict they are, particularly on whether consent is required in advance. **Check yours**, and check the rules where your recipients are, not just where you are.

## 📬 The Practical Habits

Six things that keep you compliant in spirit and safe in practice.

**1. Use a real identity.** Your real name, your real business, and a physical address. A lookalike sending domain is fine. A fake person is not.

**2. Make opting out easy and instant.** A one-line "let me know and I'll stop" is enough on a plain text email and reads better than a footer. Whatever form it takes, honour it the same day and across every domain you own.

**3. Keep a suppression record permanently.** Not per campaign. If somebody asked you to stop in 2024, they are still on it. This is also a deliverability requirement, so you would do it anyway. Section 6.7.

**4. Never disguise the nature of the message.** No "RE:" on a first email, no fabricated prior conversation, no subject line that implies something the body does not deliver.

**5. Be careful with personal addresses.** A gmail address belonging to a business owner sits in a greyer area than a company domain, because it may be treated as a personal rather than a business contact. That is a reason to be conservative with volume and frequency on bucket four, not a reason to avoid it.

**6. Handle data sensibly.** Know what you store, keep it accurate, and delete it when asked. If somebody asks what you hold on them, be able to answer.

## 🗄️ What You Are Actually Storing

Worth thinking about once, properly.

You are holding business contact details sourced from public listings, plus anything enrichment resolved. Mostly business information about businesses, which is the least sensitive category. Two places it gets less clear:

- 👤 **Owner names and personal email addresses.** These are personal data in most regimes, even when used for business purposes
- 📞 **Personal mobile numbers**, same

Practical response: keep the record minimal, keep it accurate, be able to delete on request, and do not share or sell it.

## 📮 Direct Mail Is Different

Physical mail generally sits under separate and usually looser rules than email in most places.

That does not make it unregulated, and the same habits apply: be identifiable, honour opt-outs, and keep the suppression list unified across channels. Somebody who told you to stop emailing should not then receive a card.

## ☎️ So Is the Phone

Cold calling businesses is regulated separately again, and in some jurisdictions there are registers you are expected to check before calling.

Find out whether one applies to you before you build the calling half of this. It is a ten-minute question and an expensive thing to get wrong.

## ✅ Before You Move On

- A real sender identity with a physical address
- An opt-out that is easy, instant and honoured across every domain
- A permanent suppression record
- No disguised subject lines or fabricated threads
- A check on the rules in your jurisdiction and your recipients'
- A check on calling registers before you start dialling
