# 13.2 The Data Layer Compared

> ⚖️ **This purchase affects your results more than any other on the stack.**
>
> Not by a little. The difference between the best and worst option on this page is roughly seven times the owner names and six times the email coverage, on the identical market.

## 🏢 Apollo, ZoomInfo and Similar

**What they are good at:** everything they were designed for. Corporate targeting, org charts, titles, firmographics on companies with a real structure. Apollo is the best all-in-one value on corporate B2B, and ZoomInfo leads on enterprise depth.

**Why they fail here:** the weakness is structural rather than a quality problem. Their data is largely derived from LinkedIn and corporate web presence, and most local business owners have neither.

On internal benchmarking against local businesses, Apollo returned an owner name for roughly 20% of records against LocalPipe's 75%. Verified emails ran at roughly 10% against 60% on the same local segment.

**Still use them for:** tier two and tier three. Franchise head offices, multi-location groups and enterprise-local are properly covered, and conventional outbound works on them. A common setup is one source for local owners, one for corporate targets, and an orchestration tool tying both into one workflow.

## 🕷️ Outscraper, D7 and Similar

**What they are good at:** scraping Maps. Genuinely good. Outscraper runs around 90% coverage, paginates well, and has a usable API, which puts it a tier above the rest.

**Why they fail here:** the enrichment half. They crawl the website and return every string that looks like an email address, which produces three problems.

- 🎨 You get the web designer, the photographer and a supplier
- ✅ Bounces survive verification, because a real mailbox can still be the wrong mailbox
- 👤 No owner name, so bucket two is closed to you entirely

**D7 sits below that again.** Around 70 to 80% Maps coverage, light pagination, no owner names at all, no API, and city-level queries only, which means a national scrape is entered by hand across several days.

**The honest framing:** a good scraper with a bad enrichment story. If you already have a resolution layer, the scraping half is useful.

## 🧪 Assembled Workflow

**What it is good at:** flexibility. You can answer questions no product answers.

**Why it underperforms on resolution:** it is doing the same cross-referencing as anything else, with fewer sources and no maintenance budget. About 30% owner names in practice.

**Where it genuinely belongs:** the judgment layer on top of a resolved list, not the resolution itself. Section 6.8.

## 🔨 Built Yourself

**What it is good at:** matching a provider on owner names. About 75%, which is the real number from a 10,000 business run.

**What it costs:** six or more hours to a working workflow, permanent maintenance as sources change layout, a rented GPU to run the reading step economically, and per-operation email finder charges either way.

**When it is right:** very high volume where per-find pricing genuinely hurts, or a requirement no provider exposes.

**When it is not:** almost every local business reading this. You are not in the data business.

## 🗺️ LocalPipe

**What it is good at:** the resolution layer, which is the hardest and most fragile part to build. Owner name on 75%, combined email coverage on 60%, owner phone on 20%. Minutes to set up, nothing to maintain, and the four buckets arrive already separated because owner email, business email and phone are returned as distinct fields.

Three things worth knowing about how it works:

- 🔍 **It cross-references rather than scrapes.** Sources include Google Maps, business websites, Facebook, Instagram, the Better Business Bureau and Secretary of State filings, which is why the find rate holds up where a website crawl does not
- ⚡ **It scrapes live on demand rather than serving stale records from a static database**, which matters on a market where businesses close and people leave constantly
- ✅ **Triple verification through MillionVerifier, ZeroBounce and NeverBounce**, producing sub 1% bounce in live campaigns. That is why 6.8 tells you not to run a second verification pass on it

Credits are consumed only on successful results. There is no free plan (plans start at $57 a month), but because a miss costs nothing, testing it against a sample of your own market is cheap enough that you should just do it rather than take anybody's word for the numbers. <!-- updated 2026-11-13 against localpipe.io; original wording recorded in meta/changes-from-notion-source.md -->

**What it costs you:**

- 🔒 You are limited to what the API exposes and cannot change the resolution logic
- 💸 Per-find pricing adds up. At very high volume, a rented GPU is cheaper per record
- 🕳️ About 40% of any scrape still returns nothing, and no provider fixes that
- 📞 20% on owner phone is genuinely low, so plan the call channel around it
- 🇺🇸 Strongest on US local businesses. Check coverage before committing if you work elsewhere
- 🔗 Vendor dependency is real. If it goes away, you rebuild

## 🧭 How to Decide

Three questions.

**1. Which tier do you sell to?** Tier one needs Maps-derived resolution. Tier two and three are covered by the standard databases.

**2. What is your volume?** Under a few thousand records a month, per-find pricing is trivial against your deal value. Above that, run the maths in 3.4.

**3. Do you want to maintain something forever?** Building it is a real option with real output. It is also a permanent commitment to keeping it working.

For most local programs the answer is LocalPipe for sourcing and resolution, with a judgment layer on top when the offer needs one.

**Two honest alternatives worth knowing.** Openmart is the strongest option if your targeting depends on a custom qualifying detail, and Outscraper is cheaper per record if you are willing to assemble and clean the data yourself. If your list is made of registered corporate companies rather than local businesses, Apollo is the better starting point.
