# 11.5 The Setter

> 🎧 **A setter has one job: turn a reply into a held meeting.**
>
> They do not sell, they do not quote, they do not negotiate. They call fast, qualify lightly, book, and hand over. Narrow role, and that narrowness is what makes it work.

## 🎯 What the Role Is and Is Not

> ✅ **A setter does**
>
> Call every positive reply inside the window.
>
> Confirm they are roughly a fit.
>
> Book the meeting and get it in the calendar.
>
> Run the show-up sequence.
>
> Log everything.

> ❌ **A setter does not**
>
> Pitch the offer in detail.
>
> Quote a price.
>
> Handle real objections.
>
> Run the actual meeting.
>
> Make promises about delivery.

The moment a setter starts selling, two things break. Show rate falls, because the prospect got the pitch already. And the meeting gets harder, because whoever runs it is now correcting things that were said.

## 🔄 The Workflow

Six steps, every time, no variation.

1. 🔔 **Alert fires.** Positive reply lands, setter is notified on their phone within minutes
2. 📞 **Call within the hour.** First attempt, no preparation beyond reading the reply and the record
3. 🗣️ **Open, confirm, qualify lightly.** Two or three questions, not a discovery call
4. 📅 **Book while on the phone.** Not "I'll send you some times". Put it in the calendar with them
5. ✉️ **Confirm immediately.** Calendar invite, a line in the email thread, and the show-up sequence starts. Section 11.3
6. 📝 **Log it.** Reply type, outcome, time to first attempt, what they said

If the call does not connect, two more attempts across different times of day, then a text, then it goes to the not-now list.

## 📜 The First Call

Keep it short. Ninety seconds is a good call, not a failure.

**Open:** name, where you are calling from, and why in one line.

*"Hi Dan, it's Harvey, you replied to my email about the cleaning contracts. Have you got two minutes?"*

**Confirm the interest:** repeat back what they seemed interested in, and let them correct you. That correction is the most useful information in the call.

**Qualify lightly.** Two or three questions, maximum, and only things that determine whether a meeting is worth anybody's time. Rough size, whether they currently use somebody, whether they are the decision maker.

**Book it.** Offer two specific slots inside 48 hours. Put it in the calendar while they are still on the phone.

**Close:** confirm what will happen on the call and what you will have prepared.

## 🚧 What the Setter Says When It Gets Hard

The whole point of the role is that they do not have to improvise.

- 💰 **"What does it cost?"** → "That depends on a couple of things, which is exactly what the call covers. Are you free Thursday morning?"
- 📄 **"Just send me information."** → "Happy to, and it'll make more sense with two minutes of context. Thursday or Friday?"
- 🤝 **"We already use someone."** → "Understood, most people do. The call is a comparison, not a pitch. Worth fifteen minutes?"
- 🙅 **"I'm not the right person."** → treat as a referral. Get the name, ask for an introduction

Every one of these is written down before the setter makes their first call.

## 📊 The Numbers to Hold Them To

| Metric | Target |
| --- | --- |
| ⏱️ **Time from reply to first attempt** | Under one hour in working hours |
| 📞 **Attempts per positive reply** | Two or three, across different times of day |
| 📅 **Positive reply to booked** | Around half |
| 🤝 **Booked to held** | 70 to 80% |
| ✅ **Held to qualified** | Half or better |

**The first metric is the one that matters most**, because it is the one they fully control and the one everything else depends on.

**The last metric is the guardrail.** A setter can book a lot of meetings by being loose about fit. Qualification rate is what stops that from becoming the strategy.

## 🧰 What They Need on Day One

- 📱 The phone, the alert feed, and a shared calendar
- 📇 The record in front of them. Business, category, location, owner name, the reply itself
- 📜 The written opener and the four responses above
- 🎯 A one-line description of who is a fit and who is not
- 🚪 A clear escalation route for anything outside the script

## 🤔 When a Setter Is Not the Answer

- 📉 **Under about ten positive replies a month.** Not enough volume to justify the role. Work them yourself and cap volume accordingly
- 🎯 **Your offer needs a technical conversation** on the first call, in which case the person who runs the meeting should also take the call
- 🏗️ **Your campaign is not working yet.** A setter converts replies. They cannot create them. Fix the campaign first

## ✅ Before You Move On

- The role defined narrowly, with selling explicitly out of scope
- A six-step workflow written down
- An opener and four objection responses written before the first call
- Five metrics tracked, with time to first attempt as the headline
- An honest check on whether your volume justifies the role at all
