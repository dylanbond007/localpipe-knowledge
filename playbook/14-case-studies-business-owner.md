# 14 · Case Studies

> 📊 **One program, end to end, with the numbers at every stage.**
>
> This is the worked example that runs through the whole guide, assembled in one place so you can see how the parts connect.
>
> Figures are illustrative, built from the benchmark ranges in 15.2. Your own numbers will differ, which is what day ninety is for.

## 🧽 The Business

A commercial cleaning company. Eleven staff, one metro, selling recurring contracts to other businesses.

- 🏠 **Motion:** Local to Local. They have to send a crew
- 🚐 **Target tier:** Tier one and small tier two. Dental practices, medical offices, independent professional services
- 💰 **Deal value:** £1,500 a month, so £18,000 a year
- 🎯 **Goal:** five new contracts over twelve months, or £90,000 of new annual contract value

## 🧮 Stage One: The Maths, Before Spending Anything

Working backwards from the goal. Section 3.2.

| Stage | Number |
| --- | --- |
| Contracts needed | 5 |
| Qualified conversations, at a 30% close rate | 17 |
| Meetings held, at 60% qualifying | 29 |
| Meetings booked, at a 75% show rate | 39 |
| Positive replies, at half booking | 78 |
| **Contacts to reach**, at one positive per 75 | **5,850** |

Roughly **490 contacts a month**, or **1,470 sends** on a three-email sequence.

## 🗺️ Stage Two: Does the Market Hold It?

The check that decides whether to carry on. Section 3.3.

| Stage | Number | What happened |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Businesses found | 24,000 | Full category set, decomposed properly. Not one search |
| Surviving hygiene | 17,520 | Closed, duplicated, out of category, chains removed |
| Yielding a contactable address | 10,512 | 60% combined coverage |
| After bucket expansion | 14,191 | Many businesses give two usable addresses |
| **Verified and sendable** | **7,095** | Roughly half survives verification |

**7,095 available against 5,850 needed.** It clears, with about fourteen months of first-touch volume. Green light.

**Had they queried once per city** rather than decomposing properly, the starting number would have been closer to 5,000, the verified figure under 1,500, and the honest answer would have been no. Section 6.3 is the difference between those two outcomes.

## 🏗️ Stage Three: The Build

Fifteen days, seven of them work. Section 0.5.

- **Day 1.** Four domains, twelve inboxes, into warm-up
- **Day 2.** ICP written, offer built, four sequences drafted
- **Day 3.** Tools bought
- **Day 4.** List sourced, enriched, bucketed
- **Day 5.** Campaigns built in the sequencer
- **Day 6.** Call script and follow-up written
- **Day 7.** QA. Name sample comes back at 3% error, holdout bounce test at 1.4%
- **Days 8 to 14.** Waiting. Phone and direct mail running at the A tier
- **Day 15.** Soft launch at 15% of target volume

**Monthly cost:** around £180 for infrastructure, sequencer and data.

## 📬 Stage Four: The Campaigns

Four running, which is the same offer in four framings. Section 9.5.

| Campaign | Bucket | Angle |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Evergreen, owner direct | 1 | Proximity. "Two practices on the same road" |
| Evergreen, generic with name | 2 | Forward prompt to the named owner |
| Evergreen, no name | 3 | Route density. Same day, same crew, cheaper |
| Signal | 1 and 2 | Hiring. A new front desk hire means the back office is stretched |

## 📈 Stage Five: The First Ninety Days

| Period | What happened |
| --- | --- |
| **Weeks 3 to 4** | Bounce at 1.6%. First replies. Two positives in the first week, both called inside the hour |
| **Weeks 5 to 6** | Full volume. First three meetings held. One no-show, recovered on the day-two follow-up |
| **Weeks 7 to 12** | Around 1,500 contacts reached. 19 positive replies, so one per 79. 10 meetings held. First contract signed in week nine |
| **Day 45 review** | Leading indicators on benchmark. Bucket two outperforming bucket one on reply quality, which was the surprise |
| **Day 90** | Two contracts closed. £36,000 of annual value against roughly £540 of spend and about thirty hours |

## 🔍 What Actually Moved the Numbers

Four things, in order of impact.

**1. Sourcing depth.** Pagination, query stacking and geographic decomposition turned a market that would not have supported the goal into one that comfortably did. Nothing else on this list would have mattered without it.

**2. Working all four buckets.** Roughly doubled usable contacts from the same sourcing spend. Bucket two, the generic inbox with a resolved owner name, produced reply quality close to owner-direct.

**3. Calling inside the hour.** Positive reply to booked sat around half. The two replies that were not called the same day never booked.

**4. Proximity in the opener.** The named-street version outperformed the generic version consistently. It costs nothing and no national competitor can copy it.

## ⚠️ What Went Wrong

Because it always does.

- 📉 **Bucket three underperformed** against expectations in the first month. The copy was too close to the bucket one version. Rewriting it without any personal reference fixed it
- 🕐 **Two replies went cold** in week four when nobody was watching the inbox on a Friday. That is two potential contracts, which is the single most expensive mistake in the whole program
- 🗺️ **Three early meetings were outside the sensible radius** and were declined after the call. Geography was added as a hard filter at build time rather than a preference

## 🧭 What to Take From This

- 🧮 **Do the maths and the market check before spending.** An hour that decides a quarter
- 🪣 **Bucket expansion is the cheapest volume you will ever add**
- 📞 **Speed on replies matters more than copy quality**
- 📊 **Judge at day ninety, not day thirty.** Month one produced nothing and was working perfectly
