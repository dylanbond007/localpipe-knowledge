# 5.4 The Local Signal Bench

> 📡 **A trigger is a specific event that makes a business more buyable today than it was yesterday.**
>
> The standard trigger set is built on funding rounds, headcount velocity, new-in-role moves and tech-stack changes. A business with eleven employees and no filings has none of those.
>
> This page is what replaces them.

## ✅ What Makes a Usable Local Signal

Three tests. A signal has to pass all three or it is trivia.

- 👁️ **Observable from outside.** You can find it without knowing anybody at the business
- 🕐 **Recent.** It happened in a window short enough that it is still on their mind
- 🔗 **Connected to a problem you solve.** If you cannot draw a straight line from the signal to your offer, it is a fact rather than a trigger

That third test is the one that kills most ideas. A business celebrating fifteen years in trade is observable and datable and means nothing unless you sell something to businesses hitting fifteen years.

## 🧰 The Bench

| Signal | Where you find it | What it tells you |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 🏗️ **New location or second site** | A new Maps listing under the same brand, site footer, social post | Growth, capacity strain, and a moment where systems that worked stop working |
| 📋 **Permit filings** | City and county permit portals, usually public and searchable | They are spending money on the premises right now |
| 🚚 **Fleet growth** | Vehicle photos on the site, in reviews, or on social | More vans means more jobs, more staff and more admin |
| 💼 **Hiring** | Indeed, local job boards, a careers page, a notice in the window | The strongest everyday signal. The role itself tells you the gap |
| 📣 **Ads running right now** | Meta ad library, paid results on their category and area | They have budget and are actively buying growth, so they are not saying no on principle |
| 🌐 **New or rebuilt website** | Site changes, a copyright year that just moved | They are investing in how they present, and open to adjacent spend |
| 🕸️ **No website, or one clearly untouched for years** | The record itself | A visible gap. Works as a trigger for anything that fixes presence |
| ⭐ **Review velocity change** | Review counts over time | A sharp rise means growth. A sharp fall means something broke |
| 😠 **A recent bad review** | The review feed, sorted by date | Especially about response time, missed calls or no-shows. That is a problem with a named cause |
| 🔇 **Reviews going unanswered** | The review feed | Nobody owns the reputation. Also a reliable proxy for admin overload |
| 📜 **Licence or certification renewal** | State and trade licensing registers, which are usually public | A dated compliance moment, and a reason to be in touch that week |
| 🌦️ **Weather and storm events** | Local news and weather services | Roofing, restoration, tree work and plumbing all get a demand spike and a capacity problem |
| 📅 **Seasonal window** | The calendar and the trade | HVAC before summer, landscaping in spring, retail before the holidays. Predictable and easy to schedule |
| 👤 **Ownership change** | An owner name that resolves differently from last time, or an About page rewrite | The local equivalent of new-in-role. Everything is up for review in the first few months |

## 🎯 The Signal to Offer Map

The useful discipline is that **the same data point scores the lead and writes the opening line**. One enrichment, two jobs.

| Signal | The angle it unlocks |
| --- | --- |
| Hiring an office administrator | Admin is the bottleneck. Anything that removes admin, or does the job the hire was meant to do |
| Hiring field staff | Demand is outrunning capacity. Anything that raises throughput or job value |
| A bad review about missed calls | They are losing work they already won the right to. Answering, booking, follow-up |
| Second location opening | What worked at one site breaks at two. Consistency, systems, coverage |
| Ads running | They are paying for leads. Anything that converts more of what they are already buying |
| Reviews unanswered for months | Nobody has time. A visible, checkable gap you can show them in ninety seconds |
| Storm event in their area | A demand spike they cannot staff. Capacity, scheduling, overflow |

## 🔎 What It Costs to Collect

Not all signals are equally easy. Build in this order.

- 🆓 **Free on the record.** No website, dated website, review count, unanswered reviews, location count. You already have these from sourcing, so use them first
- 🔍 **One check per record.** Ads running, recent bad reviews, hiring posts. Cheap to automate across a list, and this is where most of the value sits
- 🛠️ **Needs real tooling.** Permit filings, licence registers, weather triggers. Worth building only once a signal has proved it converts

**Start with one signal, not eleven.** Pick the one that is free or nearly free, build a campaign on it, and see whether it lifts the numbers before you build a second.

## ⏳ Signals Decay

A trigger is only a trigger inside its window. After that it is a fact.

| Signal | Useful for roughly |
| --- | --- |
| Storm or weather event | Days |
| Bad review | 2 to 4 weeks |
| Hiring post | While the post is live, plus a month |
| Ads running | While they are running |
| New location | 3 to 6 months |
| Ownership change | 3 to 6 months |
| Licence renewal | The month either side |

This is why signal campaigns run continuously on a small trickle rather than in one big batch. You are catching businesses as they enter the window, not sending to everyone who was ever in it.

## 🏁 What to Build First

If you do nothing else on this page:

1. 🥇 **Hiring.** Highest volume, easiest to check, and the role names the problem for you
2. 🥈 **Unanswered or recent bad reviews.** Free on the record, visible, and it gives you something specific to open with
3. 🥉 **Ads running.** Proves budget and intent before you spend a word on them

Those three cover most categories, cost almost nothing, and will tell you within a month whether signal campaigns are worth more effort in your market.
