# 0.2 Pick Your Motion

> 🔀 **Two minutes.**
>
> You are selling to local businesses either way. What this page settles is whether **you** are tied to a place too, because that changes about a fifth of the guide.

## 🎯 The One Question That Decides It

> **If a perfect-fit customer appeared 500 miles away, could you serve them profitably?**
>
- **No** 👉 you are running **Local → Local**
- **Yes** 👉 you are running **Business → Local**

Everything else on this page is a way of checking you answered honestly.

## ✅ Three Checks

| Check | Local → Local | Business → Local |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Delivery.** How does the work get done? | Somebody drives there | Remotely, or they self-serve |
| **Proof.** What makes a stranger believe you? | You work with businesses near them | You work with businesses like them, and near them once you map it |
| **Ceiling.** What happens when you have contacted everyone? | You run out | You open another metro on Monday |

They almost always agree. If they do not, see the tiebreaker below.

## 🏠 Local → Local

You are a local business selling to other local businesses in your own area.

- 🧹 A **commercial cleaning company** going after restaurants, dental practices and medical offices in one metro
- 🌿 A **landscaping company** going after property managers and commercial sites within an hour's drive
- 🔧 An **HVAC contractor** going after office buildings and clinics in the county
- 🔒 A **security systems installer** going after retail strips and small warehouses in the city

**What this means for you:**

- One market, decomposed properly rather than queried once
- Proximity is your single best piece of proof, and it costs nothing
- Direct mail and drop-bys work here in a way they do not for most sellers
- Your market has a floor you will hit, usually within six to eight months, which is why sizing it before you build is a real step rather than a formality

## 🌐 Business → Local

Your customers are local businesses, but you are not tied to a place.

- 💻 **Field-service software** sold to plumbing, electrical and HVAC companies nationally
- 🎓 A **coaching or training business** for trades and contractors
- 📣 An **agency** selling marketing, web or ops services to local businesses anywhere
- 🏦 A **lender, insurer or payments provider** serving small business owners

**What this means for you:**

- No territory constraint, so you go wide across metros and deep across categories rather than deep into one city
- Your market is effectively unbounded, so volume discipline is a choice you make rather than a limit imposed on you
- Proximity proof is still available, just anchored on your customers rather than on you. Build lists in a fifteen to twenty mile radius around every account you already have, so you can say "we look after someone fifteen minutes from you"
- Where your customer map is thin, lean on peer proof instead: "142 plumbing companies run this"
- Your buyer is frequently unaware your category exists at all, which changes what the first email has to do

> ⚠️ **The data problem is identical either way.**
>
> Your ICP is local even if you are not. Under 5% of local businesses appear in the standard B2B databases, and that is just as true when you are selling to them from three states away. **Section 06 applies to you unchanged.**

## ⚠️ The Mistake People Make Here

**Agencies and service businesses almost always pick wrong.**

Run a marketing agency in Denver with mostly Denver clients and it feels like Local → Local. It usually is not. Ask the 500-mile question. Could you run campaigns for a client in Tampa? Almost certainly. You are Business → Local with a local customer base, which is a different thing.

That matters because it changes real decisions:

- ❌ You would decompose one metro to ZIP level when you should go wide across many
- ❌ You would worry about a market ceiling that does not apply to you
- ❌ You would reach for proximity proof you cannot actually use

The reverse mistake is rarer and more expensive. A business that genuinely cannot service remotely, running national lists because the tooling makes it easy, generates replies it cannot turn into deliverable work.

## 🤔 If You Are Genuinely Both

Some businesses run both. A commercial cleaner franchising nationally. A trades company selling a software product on the side.

Pick the one that describes **the campaign you are about to build**, not the company.

Two motions means two campaigns, two sets of copy and usually two lists. It does not mean one hybrid campaign, which is how you end up with a message that lands for neither.

## 🧭 What You Do With Your Answer

From here on, watch for the markers:

- **Both motions.** Most of the guide. Applies to you regardless
- **L→L only.** Read it if you picked Local → Local, skip it otherwise
- **B→L differs.** The page tells you what changes

The four pages where your answer changes the most:

- **3.3** Market Size Is the Ceiling
- **6.3** Category Matching and Query Depth
- **8.3** Testing Inside a Finite Market
- **12.3** Territory, Proximity and Density

The full breakdown is in **02 · The Two Motions**. You do not need it now. Come back when a marker makes you curious.
