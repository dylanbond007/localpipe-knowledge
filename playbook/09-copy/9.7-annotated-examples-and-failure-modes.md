# 9.7 Annotated Examples and Failure Modes

> 📄 **Four worked emails, one deliberately bad one, and what separates them.**
>
> Use these as structure, not as templates. Copy them word for word and they stop working, because the thing that makes them land is that they are specific.

## 1️⃣ Bucket One, Signal-Based

**Commercial cleaning to a dental practice. The signal is a job posting.**

> **Subject:** quick question
>

> Hi Dan,
>

> Saw you're hiring a second front desk person. Usually means the practice is growing faster than the back office, and cleaning is the first thing that slips.
>

> We look after two practices on Whiteladies Road, both on evening schedules so nobody's waiting around.
>

> Worth ten minutes to see if we'd fit?
>

> Harvey
>

**Why it works:**

- 🎯 **Opener answers why me.** A job posting is public, recent, and connected to the pitch
- 🔗 **The link is drawn for them.** Hiring at the front desk to cleaning slipping. One sentence, and it is plausible
- 🏘️ **Proof is checkable and local.** A named road, two practices. They can verify it in thirty seconds
- 🪶 **Small ask.** Ten minutes, and "see if we'd fit" gives them permission to say no
- 📏 **62 words**

## 2️⃣ Bucket Two, Forward Prompt

**HVAC maintenance to a small manufacturer. Generic inbox, owner name resolved.**

> **Subject:** Mill Road unit
>

> Hi,
>

> Hoping to reach Tom, or whoever handles the building.
>

> We service the two units either side of yours on Mill Road. Both moved to a quarterly schedule after a breakdown in the middle of a heatwave, which is the expensive way to learn.
>

> If Tom's the right person, could you pass this on? If not, who should I be asking for?
>

> Harvey
>

**Why it works:**

- 👤 **Names the owner without pretending to know him.** Third person, with an out built in
- 🏘️ **Proximity proof doing real work.** Two units either side is close to unarguable
- 😬 **The problem is a story, not a claim.** A breakdown in a heatwave is vivid and true to the trade
- 📬 **Gives the reader a job they can do.** Forwarding is easier than replying, and asking who else is a second route
- 📏 **74 words**

## 3️⃣ Bucket Three, Evergreen, No Name

**Linen supply to restaurants. No owner name resolved.**

> **Subject:** Tuesday
>

> Hi,
>

> We deliver linen to four restaurants in the Old Town, all on Tuesday and Friday runs.
>

> Adding one more on the same route means we can do it cheaper than you're likely paying now, and you get the same driver each week.
>

> Is that something worth a look?
>

> Harvey
>

**Why it works:**

- 🚫 **No fake personalization.** No "hi there", no pretending to know anything about them
- 🗺️ **Location carries the whole opener.** Four restaurants, a named area, a specific schedule
- 💰 **The offer is genuinely better because of proximity.** Route density is a real reason, not a marketing claim
- 🪶 **Lowest friction ask on the list.** "Worth a look" costs nothing to answer
- 📏 **58 words**

## 4️⃣ Bucket Four, Personal Provider

**Same linen supplier, but the address is a personal one.**

> **Subject:** linen
>

> Hi Sam,
>

> We run linen deliveries to four places in the Old Town, Tuesdays and Fridays.
>

> Is that something you sort yourself, or does someone else handle it?
>

> Harvey
>

**Why it works:**

- 📱 **Reads like a message, not an email.** No preamble, no sign-off block
- ❓ **The ask is a question, not a meeting.** Far easier to answer, and any answer starts a conversation
- 🤝 **It does not pitch.** At this proximity, pitching feels like an intrusion. Asking does not
- 📏 **38 words**

## 💀 The Deliberately Bad One

Everything above, done wrong.

> **Subject:** Boost Your Restaurant's Efficiency by 40%! 🚀
>

> Hi there,
>

> I hope this email finds you well!
>

> I came across your business and was really impressed by what you're doing in the restaurant space. With 247 five-star reviews and 12 years in business, you've clearly built something special.
>

> At LinenCo, we're a leading provider of innovative linen solutions that leverage cutting-edge logistics to deliver seamless, best-in-class service to hospitality businesses nationwide. Our clients typically see a 40% improvement in operational efficiency.
>

> Would you be open to a 30-minute discovery call next week to explore how we can help you unlock your full potential? I'd love to learn more about your business!
>

> Best regards,
>

> Harvey Le
>

> Senior Account Executive | LinenCo
>

> [logo] [phone] [linkedin] [calendar link]
>

**The diagnosis:**

| Problem | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **Subject promises a number** | Reads as marketing before it is opened. The emoji finishes the job |
| **"Hi there"** | Announces that you do not know who they are |
| **"I hope this email finds you well"** | Nobody has ever written this to somebody they know |
| **247 reviews, 12 years** | Public, accurate, and creepy. Proves surveillance rather than relevance |
| **Mechanism-first paragraph** | Innovative, leverage, cutting-edge, seamless, best-in-class. Five hype words, no meaning |
| **"40% improvement in operational efficiency"** | Unverifiable, and it is not a number they measure |
| **30-minute discovery call** | Large ask, framed in your language rather than theirs |
| **Signature block** | Job title, logo, links. It is a company writing, not a person |
| **168 words** | Roughly three times too long for a phone screen |

Most of this email is not badly written. It is competently written for the wrong reader.

## ⚠️ The Five Failure Modes

Everything that goes wrong falls into one of these.

- 🕵️ **Surveillance personalization.** Reciting public facts to prove effort. Section 9.4
- 🔩 **Mechanism first.** Explaining how before they want the what. Section 9.1
- 👔 **Corporate register.** Written for an office, read on a job site
- 💥 **A merge field that blew up.** "Hi {{FirstName}}," or an empty space where a place name should be. Section 10.2
- 🪣 **Copy in the wrong bucket.** A first name in a bucket three email, or a forward prompt sent to the owner directly

## ✅ How to Use These

- Write your own version of each of the four, for your business and your market
- Run each through the could-be-anyone test from 9.4
- Read each one aloud
- Keep them in one place so the whole set stays consistent when you revise the offer

## 🏢 Agency edition differences

The text above is the Business Owner Guide version, written to the person running the program. The Agency Guide version of this chapter is identical except for the 1 passage below. Use these when the work is outbound run on behalf of a client.

1. **Under “✅ How to Use These”, in place of the passage beginning “- Write your own version of each of the…”, the agency edition reads:**

   > - Write your own version of each of the four, for the client and the market
   > - Run each through the could-be-anyone test from 9.4
   > - Read each one aloud
   > - Keep them in one place per client, so the whole set stays consistent when the offer changes
   >
   > > 🏢 **Two clients in the same trade will drift toward the same copy**, because you wrote both in the same afternoon. Local markets are small enough that a prospect can receive two near-identical emails from different senders. Write them in different weeks, or deliberately use a different angle for each.
