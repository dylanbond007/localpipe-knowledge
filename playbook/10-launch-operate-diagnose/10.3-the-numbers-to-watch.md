# 10.3 The Numbers to Watch

> 📊 **Four numbers tell you what to fix today. Four tell you whether the system is healthy.**
>
> Most people watch the wrong four and react to noise.

## ⚡ The Four Leading Numbers

Checked daily, in about five minutes. These tell you what to fix now.

| Number | Healthy | What it means when it moves |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 💥 **Bounce rate** | Under 2% | List problem, or reputation problem. Read the bounce messages to tell which. Section 4.7 |
| 📬 **Reply rate** | Against your own baseline | A drop with nothing else changed usually means placement, not copy |
| 👍 **Positive reply share** | Rising or steady | This is message-market fit. Falling means the offer or the targeting drifted |
| 🎯 **Contacts per positive reply** | 1 in 50 to 100 | The single most useful number you have. Everything upstream lands here |

## 🐢 The Four Lagging Numbers

Checked weekly or monthly. These tell you whether the machine is actually producing.

- 📞 **Reply to booked meeting.** Below half and the problem is usually response speed, not wording
- 🤝 **Show rate.** Below 70% and your confirmation layer is thin
- ✅ **Qualification rate.** Low means you are booking the wrong businesses. A targeting problem
- 💰 **Opportunities per month**, using your own definition of an opportunity. The number that actually matters

## ⏱️ The Rhythm

- 🕔 **Daily, five minutes.** The four leading numbers. Nothing else
- 📅 **Weekly, thirty minutes.** Lagging numbers, a real placement test, a sample of bounce messages, batch rotation
- 🗓️ **Monthly, one to two hours.** Full review. Scale, optimise or kill decisions. Rerun the money model against actual figures

Five minutes a day is genuinely enough when nothing is wrong. The weekly half hour is where problems get caught early, and it is the one people skip.

## 🚦 Scale, Optimise or Kill

Three states. One right move each, and the commonest mistake is doing the opposite.

> 📈 **Hitting the benchmark → Scale**
>
> Do not get clever. Send more of exactly the same thing.
>
> Expand buckets first, then adjacent categories, then geography. Section 3.3.
>
> **The mistake:** optimising a winner and breaking it.

> 🔧 **Close but under → Optimise**
>
> Work down the stack, one variable at a time.
>
> Deliverability, list, offer, copy. In that order. Section 10.4.
>
> **The mistake:** changing four things at once and learning nothing.

> 🛑 **Well under, after a full test → Kill**
>
> Stop sending. A campaign that is badly underperforming is not just failing to produce, it is consuming a finite market and generating complaints.
>
> **The mistake:** adding a fourth email to a campaign that three could not carry.

## 💬 Negative Replies Are Data

The instinct is to delete them. They are the cheapest market research available.

- 🚫 **"We already have someone"** → the market is served, so your angle needs to be about switching rather than solving
- 💸 **"Too expensive"** at volume → a positioning problem, not a pricing one
- 🤷 **"Not relevant"** repeatedly → a targeting problem. Go back to 5.1
- 😠 **Genuinely annoyed** → check your suppression and how recently you contacted that market

Log the reason on every negative reply. After fifty you will have a clearer picture of your market than any research would give you.

## 🏪 One Local-Specific Warning

Your market is finite, which changes what a bad month costs.

In a large market, a poorly performing campaign wastes budget. Here it also **consumes contacts you cannot get back for months**. That makes killing a bad campaign more urgent than it would be elsewhere, and it makes the daily five minutes worth more than it looks.

## ✅ Before You Move On

- The four leading numbers visible daily
- A reply-rate baseline recorded so you can spot a drop
- A weekly slot booked, with a real placement test in it
- A decision rule for scale, optimise or kill, written down in advance
- Negative reply reasons being logged

## 🏢 Agency edition differences

The text above is the Business Owner Guide version, written to the person running the program. The Agency Guide version of this chapter is identical except for the 2 passages below. Use these when the work is outbound run on behalf of a client.

1. **Under “⚡ The Four Leading Numbers”, in place of the passage beginning “Checked daily, in about five minutes. These tell you…”, the agency edition reads:**

   > Checked daily, in about five minutes per client. You are looking for anomalies rather than reading detail.

2. **Under “🐢 The Four Lagging Numbers”, after the passage beginning “- 📞 Reply to booked meeting. Below half and…”, the agency edition adds:**

   > > 🏢 **Only one of these eight belongs in a client report.**
   > >
   > > The leading numbers are how you diagnose. The lagging numbers, and opportunities in particular, are what you report. Send a client a bounce rate and a send count and you have invited them to judge you on activity. A2.5 covers the five-line report.
