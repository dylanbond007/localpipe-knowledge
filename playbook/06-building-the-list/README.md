# 06 · Building the List

> 🗺️ **What this section does.** The longest and most important section, and the one most people came for. Google Maps is a consumer directory being used as a sales database. It holds the business, the category, the address, the phone and usually a website. It does not hold an email address and it does not hold an owner name. Everything hard follows from those two missing fields.

**Applies to:** Both motions. The data layer does not care who you are. **B→L differs** only on 6.3, where you go wide across metros rather than deep into one.

> 🤝 **Co-authored with Dylan Bond, founder of LocalPipe.** The find rates and provider comparisons in this section come from a controlled test across four data providers and an analysis of roughly 7.2 million emails sent to local businesses.

## Chapters

1. **6.1 Why the databases you know fail on local** — coverage and saturation, and what each does to market size
2. **6.2 Sourcing from Google Maps** — what a raw record holds, and the five-stage loop
3. **6.3 Category matching and query depth** — 4,000 categories, and the three techniques that multiply your market
4. **6.4 The four contact buckets** — owner direct, generic with a name, generic without, and the personal provider address the owner reads themselves
5. **6.5 Generic and free-provider inboxes in practice** — why deleting either removes most of your reachable market
6. **6.6 Owner identification and enrichment** — the anchor chapter. Cross-referencing, the waterfall, find rates, and where cheap tools break
7. **6.7 Hygiene, coverage math and pre-send QA** — filter before you enrich, size in usable sends, and the checks that have each cost somebody a campaign
8. **6.8 Building it** — the LocalPipe interface, the API driven from Claude Code, and the judgment enrichment layer in Clay
