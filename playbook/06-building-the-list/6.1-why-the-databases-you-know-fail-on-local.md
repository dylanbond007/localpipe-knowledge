# 6.1 Why the Databases You Know Fail on Local

> 🕳️ **The tools everyone uses to build B2B lists were built for companies with LinkedIn pages, funding rounds and org charts.**
>
> A twelve-person plumbing company has none of those. As far as those databases are concerned, it does not exist.
>
> This is not a small gap. It is the reason local outbound is a different job.

## 📉 Failure One: Coverage

The businesses simply are not in there.

- 🕳️ **Under 5%** of local small and medium businesses are covered by the major B2B databases
- 🔧 **80 to 90%** of home service businesses are missing from them entirely
- 🗺️ **3 to 20 times** higher addressable coverage from Maps-derived sourcing, depending on the trade

Think about what that means when you build a list. You pick your filters, you run the search, you get 200 plumbers in your metro, and you plan a campaign around 200 plumbers.

There are probably several thousand. You are not looking at a market. You are looking at a sample, and it was never a representative one.

## 🔁 Failure Two: Saturation

This is the one people miss, and it is worse than the first.

The small pool those databases *do* hold is the same pool every other operator working your vertical finds. Same filters, same exports, same sequences. Those records get contacted over and over.

**Positive replies ran about 1.6 times higher for contacts that were not in the big database.** Same offer, same infrastructure, same copy. The only difference was where the contact came from.

The reason is simple and human:

> **Somebody who gets four cold emails a week has learned to ignore them. Somebody who gets four a year has not.**
>

Data exclusivity affects performance, not just coverage. Two operators can run identical campaigns into the identical category and get very different results, purely because one of them is working a list nobody else has.

## 📊 Same Business, Same Metro, Two Data Sources

A commercial cleaning company selling into one metropolitan area, targeting dental practices, medical offices and independent professional services.

| Stage | Corporate database | Maps-derived sourcing |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Businesses returned in the metro | 340 | 2,900 |
| Records with a contactable address | 310 | 1,220 |
| Records with an identified owner name | 95 | 915 |
| Share previously contacted heavily | High | Low |
| Months of program volume supported | Under one | Six to eight |

Same business, same geography, same offer. One version cannot sustain a program and the other can, and the entire difference sits upstream of anything a copywriter does.

Figures are illustrative, shaped to the ratios in the underlying comparison.

## 🧠 What This Should Change in Your Head

Three things, and they run against instinct.

- 🚫 **An empty result is not an empty market.** If the filters return almost nothing, that is a statement about the database, not about your opportunity
- 🎯 **Better data is a multiplier, not a tidy-up.** It raises coverage *and* reply rate at the same time, which almost nothing else in this guide does
- 🥇 **The list is the lever.** In corporate outbound, copy and offer usually decide the outcome because everybody has the same data. Here, the data is the variable

## 🏬 Where the Old Databases Still Win

This is not an argument that those tools are bad. They are excellent at what they were built for, and there are two local cases where you should still use them.

- 🏢 **Franchise head offices and multi-location groups.** Real corporate structures, real titles, real LinkedIn presence. Everything the standard databases are good at
- 🏭 **Enterprise-local.** Large commercial, industrial, facilities and construction. These companies are properly covered, and conventional outbound works on them

So the split is by tier rather than by tool. Owner-operators and small SMBs need Maps-derived sourcing. Everything above that is reachable the ordinary way. Section 0.3.

## ✅ Before You Move On

- An understanding that your database result is a fraction of the real market
- The reason exclusivity lifts reply rate, not just list size
- A clear view of which tier you are targeting, and therefore which source is right
- No more sizing a market from a database export
