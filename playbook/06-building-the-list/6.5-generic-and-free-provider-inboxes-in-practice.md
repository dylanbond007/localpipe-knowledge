# 6.5 Generic and Free-Provider Inboxes in Practice

> 🧠 **Two rules that are correct everywhere except here.**
>
> Delete the role addresses. Delete the free providers. Follow both on a local list and you have just removed most of the contacts that actually reach a decision maker.

## 1️⃣ A Generic Inbox Is Not a Queue at This Size

The advice to delete info@ is correct at corporate scale. Every condition behind it needs organisational scale to be true.

> 🏢 **At a company of any size**
>
> A role address is a queue.
>
> Monitored by somebody junior. Full of vendor solicitation. The person reading it has no authority to buy anything. Messages get triaged into a ticketing system rather than read.

> 🏪 **At an eleven-person landscaping company**
>
> There is nobody to delegate that inbox to.
>
> It is read by the owner, or the owner's spouse, or an office manager who has been there nine years, knows exactly what the owner cares about, and forwards anything that looks like revenue or a problem.

Every condition that makes the corporate rule true is absent. So the conclusion does not hold.

**In many verticals, generic inbox performance was close to equal with owner direct. In some, generic outperformed.**

## 📮 Which Role Addresses to Keep

| Address pattern | Verdict |
| --- | --- |
| info@, contact@, hello@, office@ | ✅ Keep |
| admin@, accounts@, bookings@ | ✅ Usually keep |
| sales@, enquiries@ | ✅ Keep |
| support@, help@ | ⚠️ Case by case |
| A named employee who is not the owner | ⚠️ Case by case |
| noreply@, donotreply@ | ❌ Drop |
| webmaster@, postmaster@, abuse@ | ❌ Drop |

The pattern is simple. Keep anything a human reads. Drop anything built for machines or for complaints.

## 2️⃣ Free Provider Addresses Get Isolated, Not Deleted

Standard practice says delete gmail and outlook addresses. In a local market that removes a large share of the contacts that reach an actual decision maker.

- 💼 **On a corporate record**, a free provider address usually means bad data
- 🏪 **On a local record**, it means the owner personally, about nine times out of ten

A business started in 2009 by one person with a truck set up a free address, the business grew, and nobody ever saw a reason to migrate. Nobody handed that inbox to an office manager. It is still the owner's phone buzzing, which is why it is its own bucket rather than a flag on the others.

**The numbers:** bounce runs about **32% higher** on this segment. If the rest of your list bounces at 1.2%, this bounces near 1.6%. That is uncomfortable mixed into everything else and completely workable on its own.

Which is the whole point. **Isolate it, do not delete it.** It is bucket four in 6.4, and how to run it as its own lane, with parked domains and rotating batches, is 4.6.

## 🗑️ What Deleting Both Actually Costs

Run both standard rules on a typical local list and you remove:

- Every role address, which is usually the largest bucket by volume
- Every free provider address, which is often where the owner personally sits

**A third to a half of your reachable market, and frequently the better-performing half.** On a market that 3.3 already told you is finite, that is not tidying. That is cutting your program in half to make a report look neat.

## 🔍 Telling a Person From a Role

Useful when enrichment gives you an address with no name attached.

- 👤 **Personal format:** firstname@, firstname.lastname@, initials@. Almost always a person, and usually the owner at this size
- 🏢 **Role format:** the patterns in the table above. A function rather than a human
- 🤔 **Ambiguous:** a first name that is also a word. mike@ is a person. sales@ is not. jade@ could be either, depending on the business

When you cannot tell, treat it as a role address and write bucket three copy. Getting that wrong in the safe direction costs you a little personalization. Getting it wrong the other way means addressing a company as if it were a person.

## ⚠️ What Not to Do With This

Three ways people take this too far.

- 🚫 **Do not scrape every address off the website.** That is not enrichment. You get the web designer, the photographer, a supplier and something left in a stock template. 6.6 covers why this fails
- 🚫 **Do not send bucket one and bucket three copy to the same inbox.** One address, one campaign, one voice
- 🚫 **Do not treat this as permission to email six addresses per business.** Two to three routes, one persona. 6.4

## ✅ Before You Move On

- Role addresses kept, with only the machine addresses dropped
- Free provider addresses flagged and segmented rather than deleted
- Ambiguous addresses defaulted to bucket three
- An honest estimate of how much of your market these two rules would have removed
