# 04 · Deliverability

> 📫 **What this section does.** Gets the mail into the primary inbox and keeps it there. Standard infrastructure first, then the two things local does to it: a stricter bounce bar, and a large segment of personal-provider addresses that cannot be warmed and must not be deleted.

**Applies to:** Both motions. The free-provider lane is heavier on **L→L** because the lists skew further toward one-van operators, but the mechanics are identical.

**Do this first.** Order infrastructure on day one. It warms for two to three weeks while you build everything else.

## Chapters

1. **4.1 What deliverability is, and who judges your mail** — authentication, reputation, placement, ESPs and gateways
2. **4.2 Domains, inboxes and DNS** — lookalikes, registrars, cheap versus premium, SPF/DKIM/DMARC, why to outsource
3. **4.3 Sizing your sending estate** — the arithmetic from monthly sends to inboxes and domains, with headroom
4. **4.4 Warm-up and ramp** — what warming is, the ratios, the ramp schedules
5. **4.5 The 2% bar, and infrastructure by contact bucket** — the single bounce threshold, and separating infrastructure by bucket
6. **4.6 The free-provider lane** — why you cannot follow the easy-inbox rule on a local list, and what to do instead
7. **4.7 What to monitor, and reading bounces** — the daily and weekly rhythm, and the bounce message table

## 🏢 Agency edition differences

The text above is the Business Owner Guide version, written to the person running the program. The Agency Guide version of this chapter is identical except for the 2 passages below. Use these when the work is outbound run on behalf of a client.

1. **At the top of the chapter, in place of the passage beginning “Applies to: Both motions. The free-provider lane is heavier…” through “Do this first. Order infrastructure on day one. It…”, the agency edition reads:**

   > **Applies to:** Both motions. The free-provider lane is heavier on **L→L clients**, because those lists skew further toward one-van operators, but the mechanics are identical.
   >
   > **Do this first, and hold spare capacity.** Order infrastructure on day one of any engagement. It warms for two to three weeks, which is the single biggest friction in selling a local program. An agency holding a rotating pool of pre-warmed inboxes launches a client in days instead.
