# 4.4 Warm-Up and Ramp

> 🔥 **Warm-up and ramp are two different things, and almost everyone skips the second one.**
>
> A warmed mailbox lasts three months or more. An un-warmed one often dies in two to four weeks. Send full volume on day one and you kill inboxes **5 to 10 times faster**.

## 🤔 What Warming Up Actually Is

Gradually mimicking real human sending so your inboxes do not look like spam cannons on day one.

Two parties need convincing:

- 📤 **The sending ESP**, which hosts your inboxes and is deciding whether you are a legitimate customer or a problem
- 📥 **The receiving ESP**, which hosts your prospect's inbox and is deciding whether to trust mail from your domain

Warm-up tools do this by sending mail between a pool of real mailboxes that open, reply and mark as important. The receiving systems see engagement, and the reputation builds.

**14 to 21 days** per inbox before it carries real volume.

Three things people get wrong about it:

- ⛓️ **Domain age does not transfer to mailbox reputation.** An aged domain still needs every new mailbox warmed from scratch
- ♾️ **Warm-up never fully stops.** It runs alongside your cold sending permanently, at a lower ratio
- ⚖️ **Warm-up emails should outnumber cold ones.** That ratio is the whole mechanism

## 📊 The Golden Ratio

| Platform | Cold per day | Warm-up per day | Ratio warm to cold | Warm-up reply rate |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 🟢 **Google Workspace** | 15 | About 25 | 1 : 1.75 | 60% |
| 🟠 **Microsoft 365** | 10 | About 20 | 1 : 2 | 50 to 60% |
| 🔵 **Azure** (per mailbox) | 2 | 5 (hard maximum) | 5 is the ceiling | 60%+ |

## 🚀 Ramp Is the Other Half

Warm-up proves the mailbox is real. **Ramp** is how you introduce cold volume once it is. They are separate, and skipping ramp undoes the warm-up you just paid two weeks for.

- 🟢 **Google.** Start at 2 to 3 cold per day, add 2 to 3 per day, reach about 15 over roughly four weeks
- 🟠 **Microsoft 365.** Start at 2 to 3 per day, add 2 per day, reach about 10 over roughly four weeks
- 🔵 **Azure.** Start at 1 per day, add 1 per day, reach the ceiling of 5 over five to seven days

So the full timeline from buying a mailbox to running it at full speed is **six weeks or so**, not two. Plan for that. Section 0.5 builds it into the schedule.

> ⚠️ **These numbers are a current baseline, not physics.**
>
> Warm-up and ramp protocols change quarterly, sometimes monthly. Get updates from whoever sold you the mailboxes, especially at the point of purchase. If a provider cannot tell you their current protocol, that tells you something.

## 🏪 If Your Audience Is on Personal Email

This is the local problem, and it is worth seeing here before you meet it properly in 4.6.

Plenty of local ICPs receive business mail at personal addresses. Contractors, one-van operators, small SMBs. Expect infrastructure to **burn 3 to 5 times faster**, for two reasons:

- 🛡️ Personal inboxes run aggressive consumer spam filters
- 🚫 **Sequencers cannot warm against personal Gmail.** No warm-up signal exists there, so every send you make into that segment looks cold

You still warm for the full two weeks, because that proves out the sending side even when it cannot prove out the receiving side.

What you do not do is mix that segment into your main estate. Section 4.6.

## ⏱️ What This Means for Your Timeline

| When | What is happening |
| --- | --- |
| **Day 1** | Buy domains and mailboxes. Warm-up starts immediately |
| **Days 1 to 14** | Warm-up only. No cold sending at all. Build everything else during this window |
| **Day 15** | First cold sends, at 2 to 3 per inbox per day |
| **Weeks 3 to 6** | Ramp. Climb toward 15 per inbox per day while watching bounce and placement |
| **Week 6 onward** | Steady state. Warm-up still running alongside, permanently |

This is why 0.5 tells you to buy infrastructure on day one before you have decided anything else. It is the only part of the build with a clock you cannot speed up.

## 🚨 What Skipping It Looks Like

Not theoretical. This is the sequence:

1. Mailboxes go live at full volume because warm-up felt like a delay
2. First week looks fine. Nothing bounces, a couple of replies come in
3. Week two, placement quietly shifts to spam. You cannot see this. Reply rate falls
4. Week three, bounces climb and the sending ESP flags the account
5. Week four, mailboxes are dead. You have burned four domains, two weeks of sending and your first impression on a market you cannot re-contact for six months

The infrastructure cost of that is maybe sixty dollars. The real cost is the market, because those contacts have now been emailed badly by you and 3.3 already told you how few of them there are.

## ✅ Before You Move On

- Every mailbox in warm-up for 14 to 21 days before it sends anything cold
- Warm-up ratio set correctly for your lane, and left running permanently
- A ramp schedule, not just a warm-up schedule
- Six weeks planned from purchase to full volume
- The free-provider segment kept out of this estate entirely
