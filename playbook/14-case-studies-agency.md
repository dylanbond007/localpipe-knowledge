# 14 · Case Studies

> 📊 **One client engagement, end to end, with the numbers at every stage.**
>
> This is the worked example that runs through the guide, assembled in one place so you can see how the parts connect and where the commercial decisions sit.
>
> Figures are illustrative, built from the benchmark ranges in 15.2.

## 🧽 The Client

A commercial cleaning company. Eleven staff, one metro, selling recurring contracts to other businesses.

- 🏠 **Motion:** Local to Local. They have to send a crew
- 🚐 **Their targets:** Tier one and small tier two. Dental practices, medical offices, independent professional services
- 💰 **Their deal value:** £1,500 a month, so £18,000 a year
- 🎯 **What they asked for:** five new contracts over twelve months

## 🔍 Stage One: Qualification, Before You Quote

Five questions from A1.3, on the first call.

- ☎️ **Who calls back?** The office manager, in the building, all day. Strong answer
- 🎯 **Has this offer closed before?** Yes, eleven existing contracts won by referral
- 🗺️ **Can they service it?** Forty-five minute radius, capacity for six more contracts
- 🔥 **Burned this market?** No previous outbound
- 📊 **What is a good month?** They said two new contracts. **The maths said 0.4.** That gap was the most important thing surfaced on the call

That last one is the conversation most agencies avoid and it is why this engagement survived to month six.

## 🧮 Stage Two: The Maths, Before the Proposal

| Stage | Number |
| --- | --- |
| Contracts needed | 5 |
| Qualified conversations, at 30% close | 17 |
| Meetings held, at 60% qualifying | 29 |
| Meetings booked, at 75% show | 39 |
| Positive replies, at half booking | 78 |
| **Contacts to reach** | **5,850** |

About **490 contacts a month**, or **1,470 sends** on a three-email sequence.

## 🗺️ Stage Three: Does the Market Hold It?

| Stage | Number |
| --- | --- |
| Businesses found, full category set, decomposed | 24,000 |
| Surviving hygiene | 17,520 |
| Yielding a contactable address | 10,512 |
| After bucket expansion | 14,191 |
| **Verified and sendable** | **7,095** |

**7,095 available against 5,850 needed**, or about fourteen months of first-touch volume.

That paragraph went on page one of the proposal, along with the named expansion path. It is the reason month eleven was a planned conversation rather than an argument.

## 🏗️ Stage Four: The Build

Live on **day eight**, not day fifteen, because the inboxes came from a pre-warmed pool.

- **Day 1.** Domains bought and parked, client's lookalikes. Warm pool allocated
- **Day 2.** ICP built on the onboarding call. Client named three "best customers" who turned out to be their three largest and two of the three were marginally profitable
- **Day 3 to 4.** List sourced, enriched, bucketed. Suppression loaded from their customer export
- **Day 5 to 6.** Campaigns and the call script. Pre-call page built for their office manager
- **Day 7.** QA. Name sample 3% error, holdout bounce 1.4%
- **Day 8.** Soft launch at 15%

## 📈 Stage Five: The First Ninety Days

| Period | What happened |
| --- | --- |
| **Weeks 1 to 2** | Bounce 1.6%. First replies. Time to first call averaging 25 minutes |
| **Weeks 3 to 4** | Full volume. Three meetings held. One no-show recovered on the day-two follow-up |
| **Weeks 5 to 12** | 1,500 contacts. 19 positive replies, one per 79. 10 meetings held. First contract in week nine |
| **Day 45 review** | Leading indicators on benchmark. Bucket two outperforming bucket one, which was the surprise |
| **Day 90** | Two contracts closed. £36,000 of annual value |

## 🔍 What Actually Moved the Numbers

**1. Sourcing depth.** Pagination, stacking and decomposition turned a market that would not have supported the goal into one that comfortably did. Nothing else mattered without it.

**2. All four buckets.** Roughly doubled usable contacts from the same sourcing spend. Bucket two produced reply quality close to owner-direct.

**3. Twenty-five minute callbacks.** The office manager was the single biggest variable in the engagement, and it was established at onboarding rather than discovered later.

**4. Proximity in the opener.** The named-street version consistently beat the generic one, and no national competitor could copy it.

## ⚠️ What Went Wrong

- 📉 **Bucket three underperformed** in month one. The copy was too close to the bucket one version. Rewriting it without any personal reference fixed it
- 🗺️ **Three early meetings were outside the radius** and declined after the call. Geography went in as a hard filter rather than a preference
- 📊 **The client asked about emails sent** in month two, because the first report included it. It came out of the report and the conversation moved back to opportunities

## 🧭 What to Take From This

- 🔍 **The qualification call saved the engagement.** The gap between "two contracts a month" and 0.4 was surfaced before a proposal existed
- 📄 **The market number on page one** made month eleven a planned conversation
- ⚡ **Day eight instead of day fifteen** came from holding pre-warmed capacity, and it was the thing the client mentioned most in the first fortnight
- 📞 **The callback speed did more than the copy.** It usually does
