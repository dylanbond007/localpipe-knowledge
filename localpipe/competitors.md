# LocalPipe against the alternatives

LocalPipe's own comparison pages name where competitors are the better choice. Keep that tone. A comparison that only flatters LocalPipe is off-brand and less persuasive.

**The honest summary:** LocalPipe wins on local owner data, and loses on corporate contacts and all-in-one platform features.

Competitor prices below were taken from each vendor's published plans and last verified by LocalPipe on 12 November 2026. Vendors change pricing often. Check before quoting.

### Each tool in one block

**LocalPipe.** Covers 100% of Google Maps and is built to identify the owner behind a listing. 75% owner name, 60% email on local businesses. Pay on find, unlimited seats, API on every plan. *Weakness:* a data layer, not a sales platform. No sequencing, no dialer, no CRM, no pre-built index of people. Wrong tool for reaching a job title inside a larger company.

**Openmart.** An AI-driven local lead platform that screens each business against criteria you describe in plain language, such as whether a clinic offers a particular treatment. The custom AI qualification is the real differentiator and removes a manual research step. Basic business info exports cost no credits. *Weakness:* paid plans start higher at $149/month, credits go quickly on the contact data you want (owner emails 3 credits, direct phones 8), coverage is centred on the US, and it is a narrower product. **Strongest alternative if targeting depends on a custom qualifying detail.**

**Outscraper.** Pay-as-you-go scraping with separate tasks for Maps data, email extraction, validation and phone enrichment. Best unit economics for raw business records, around $3 per 1,000 for base Maps data, no monthly commitment, a straightforward API that scales. *Weakness:* you buy tasks, not outcomes. A usable lead means stacking tasks, which lands around $9 to $14 per 1,000 records, and you pay for rows whether or not a contact was found. Emails are extracted from websites rather than attributed to a named owner, so you often get info@. In the October 2026 benchmark 36% of its returned emails were invalid and 21% catch-all. **Cheaper per record if you only need business data and will clean the output yourself.**

**Clay.** An orchestration platform that chains enrichment providers into a waterfall and runs AI research steps. Nothing else matches its flexibility, and LocalPipe is commonly used as a source inside it through the API and ready-made template. *Weakness:* Clay supplies orchestration, not local business data. Waterfall results are only as good as the providers connected, and most mainstream providers are weak on owner-operated local businesses. Real learning curve, a dual credit and action model that makes spend hard to predict, $167/month on annual Launch. DIY Clay flows for owner data take 6+ hours to build and land around 30% owner names and 14% emails.

**Apollo.io.** Over 240 million contacts across 30 million companies, with sequencing, a dialer, intent data and bidirectional CRM sync. The most complete platform here and the best value for classic B2B outbound. *Weakness:* thin on owner-operated local businesses because the data is largely LinkedIn-derived. Roughly 20% owner names and 10% emails on local businesses in LocalPipe's internal benchmarking. No Google Maps search, per-seat pricing, credits do not roll over.

**ZoomInfo.** The best depth and accuracy on mid-market and enterprise accounts: org charts, verified direct dials, intent data, compliance posture. *Weakness:* by far the most expensive, annual contracts with a three-seat minimum, typically from $15,000 a year and often $30,000 to $60,000 with seats and add-ons. No monthly billing, no permanent free plan. Coverage of local owner-operated businesses does not justify the cost.

**Lusha.** The easiest to start with, a good browser extension, strong accuracy on direct dials for the contacts it holds, lowest entry price of the paid platforms, and it plugs into Clay waterfalls. *Weakness:* a smaller, LinkedIn-centric database with limited local owner coverage. Built for per-contact lookups, with no Google Maps search or local business discovery.

**LeadSwift, Seamless, Hunter.** LeadSwift appears in the homepage table (low owner name and email rates, no owner phones, volume caps). Seamless and Hunter appear in the October 2026 benchmark: 7.0% and 4.5% verified sendable coverage on plumbing businesses. Seamless was the cleanest of the traditional databases at 87% valid. Hunter's domain search works best where a company publishes addresses on its own domain, which small trades businesses rarely do.

## LocalPipe vs Apollo in detail

Apollo is the tool buyers most often compare LocalPipe to. The site's position: **Apollo is the better product for most B2B sales teams. LocalPipe is the better product for one specific job: finding the owner of a local business that Apollo cannot see.** They are not really substitutes. The right answer is decided almost entirely by who you sell to.

### Pick LocalPipe if

- Your list is local and owner-operated: trades, clinics, restaurants, retail, home services
- The person you need is the owner, and they are not reliably on LinkedIn
- You want to build lists from a geography and a category rather than firmographic filters
- You already have a sending tool, or you run enrichment through Clay or your own API workflow
- You want billing tied to successful finds rather than to seats

### Pick Apollo if

- You sell to registered companies with a real LinkedIn and web footprint
- You need job titles, seniority, headcount, technographics or intent signals to segment
- You want prospecting, sequencing and dialing in one platform
- You need native bidirectional sync with Salesforce or HubSpot without building it
- You want to evaluate the data at no cost before committing budget

If both descriptions match parts of the book, running both is a reasonable and common outcome.

### Buyer questions and the site's answers

- **Which has the bigger database?** It depends on whether you count businesses or people. Bigger business universe goes to LocalPipe (roughly 250M on Google Maps by third-party estimate against 30M companies). Bigger contact index goes to Apollo (240M+ people with titles).
- **Can Apollo find local owner emails?** Sometimes, but coverage is thin. Read the 20% and 10% figures as segment-specific, not a verdict on Apollo's overall data quality.
- **Is LocalPipe cheaper?** It depends on team size and volume. Apollo's cost rises with headcount, LocalPipe's with data volume. A three-person team enriching heavily usually pays less on LocalPipe. A single user at low volume often pays less on Apollo, and can trial it free.
- **Does LocalPipe replace Apollo?** Only if the entire market is local businesses and a sending tool is already in place.
- **Do teams run both?** Yes, commonly. Apollo for corporate and mid-market segments, LocalPipe for local owner-operated accounts. Because LocalPipe bills on finds and not per seat, adding it is usually a small incremental cost rather than a platform migration.
- **API access?** LocalPipe includes API access on every plan with webhook callbacks and a Clay template. Apollo's API availability and request volume depend on plan and credit allocation.

## Which tools have a free tier

Openmart, Apollo, Clay and Lusha have free plans. Outscraper is pay as you go with no monthly commitment. **LocalPipe has no free plan.** If evaluating at zero cost matters most, the site's own advice is to test your target list on a free plan elsewhere and check whether coverage is good enough before paying.

## How to test tools against a market

Take 50 to 100 real target businesses, run the identical list through two or three candidates, and compare three things: how many records returned a named decision maker, how many emails survived validation, and what you paid per usable contact rather than per row. Vendor benchmarks, LocalPipe's included, are measured on samples chosen by the vendor.
